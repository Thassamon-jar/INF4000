# The INF4000 Project
## Overview
The report explores strategies to enhance York Council's online services, aiming to increase awareness and encourage residents to shift from in-person to digital platforms. This transition seeks to provide greater convenience for citizens while reducing staff workloads. The initiative aligns with York Council's digital transformation goals and the UK Government's broader policy to leverage innovation for improving public services, as emphasized in the 2022 report on adopting online platforms for efficiency and accessibility.

## Research Questions
1.	What is the preferred mode of access for users?
2.	Which activities are most popular on the platform?
3.	Which devices are most commonly used?
4.	How does the intensity of activities performed online vary across different age groups?

## Key Findings
<img src="./Bar Chart.png" width="100%" height="100%">

### Graph 1

Graph 1 is a bar chart illustrating the number of respondents using various methods to access council services. The data highlights that online services are the dominant platform, with a significantly higher number of users compared to in-person, postal, or telephone methods.

The recommended strategy based on this analysis is a "keep and expand" approach. This involves maintaining the current online user base while encouraging users of traditional methods to transition to digital platforms. Simultaneously, the council should ensure accessibility for those unfamiliar with online services, maintaining inclusivity. 


<img src="./Pie Chart.png" width="100%" height="100%">

### Graph 2

Graph 2, a pie chart, illustrates the distribution of activities performed via online services. The most popular activity is "Looking for information," followed by "Paying for services" and "Reporting issues," while "Watching webcasts" is the least popular.

The key insight is the need to prioritize improvements to the "Looking for information" function to enhance user experience. Subsequent enhancements should target "Paying for services" and "Reporting issues." To encourage broader adoption of online services, the council should consider redesigning the platform's UX/UI for a more seamless and user-friendly experience, fostering greater convenience and accessibility.


<img src="./Stacked Bar Chart.png" width="100%" height="100%">

### Graph 3

Graph 3, a stacked bar chart, shows the percentage of device usage by age group for accessing council services. Computers are the most frequently used devices across all age groups, followed by mobile phones and tablets, despite the widespread ownership of mobile devices.

This preference for computers may stem from factors like non-optimized web pages for mobile screens or the nature of tasks requiring precision. For critical activities or important communications with the council, residents may favor computers to ensure accuracy and reduce the likelihood of errors. This insight suggests the need to optimize the platform for mobile use while maintaining robust functionality across all devices.


<img src="./Heat Map.png" width="100%" height="100%">

### Graph 4

The heatmap visualizes the intensity of service activities by different age groups. It highlights that the majority of users are within the 25–39 and 40–55 age groups, with older users engaging less frequently and younger individuals rarely using the service.

Key insights include differences in activity preferences among these groups. Users aged 25–39 primarily focus on specific activities, such as "Looking for information" and "Paying for services," while users aged 40–55 participate in a broader range of activities, albeit with less intensity compared to the younger cohort. These findings suggest a need to tailor online services to better meet the diverse needs of each age group.
